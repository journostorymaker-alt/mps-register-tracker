How this works

  • Data comes from the official House of Commons register API (interests-api.parliament.uk). Party and constituency come from the Parliament Members API. A scheduled job checks for a new register twice a week and rebuilds this page.
  • Party is each MP's current party. MPs who have switched parties are counted under their new one. So money a defector took as a Conservative counts as Reform UK. Labour and Labour (Co-op) are counted together.
  • "Per MP" divides the money taken by a party's sitting MPs by its number of sitting MPs in our data. Former MPs are left out of that measure.
  • Why some entries are years old: the register lists what is live, not what is new. One-off gifts, donations, trips and payments drop off 12 months after they are registered. Ongoing roles stay on for as long as they last.
  • Running totals: because old items drop off, the job also downloads every past entry, expired ones included. Each entry is counted once.
  • One-off items are counted on the date received. If there's no date, the date registered is used.
  • Undated items declared just after the election are kept out of the running total. New MPs had to declare money from the year before, and returning MPs declared their campaign funding. Many of those entries, mostly donations through local parties, have no date received. So they may pre-date polling day. They are shown as a separate figure and are still in the Search tab, marked "Just after election".
  • Regular payments (monthly, quarterly or yearly) are spread across the months the agreement ran since the election, up to its end date or the latest register. If an agreement has gone from the register and has no end date, it is taken to stop at the last register it appeared in. This is an estimate. Check big regular payments against the source.
  • Where an agency pays the MP for someone else, the true payer is shown.
  • Payer names are tidied so spelling variants count as one. Two merges are judgement calls: the Sainsbury name variants, and Fran Perrin with Francesca Perrin.
  • Sectors are a rough keyword sort. Treat them as a guide.
  • "To charity" means the MP says the money went to charity. It is still counted in totals.
